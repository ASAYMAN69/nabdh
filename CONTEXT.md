Population Health · Challenge 04

Precision Prevention and Screening
The Challenge

How might we identify children, adults and communities with unmet or elevated health risk and connect them to the right preventive intervention, Well-Child Visit or Adult comprehensive screening pathway, at the right time, through approaches that are personalized and culturally appropriate?

The Challenge

Abu Dhabi’s strategic direction is to move from reactive care to predictive prevention and early detection across the life course, using population health intelligence to convert longitudinal health and genomic data into action. Today, eligibility and outreach for the Well-Child Visit program, adult comprehensive screening and other preventive pathways can still depend heavily on broad age- and gender-based rules, while clinically important risk is distributed across family history, genomic information, chronic conditions, developmental and behavioral factors, screening and visit history, geography, and social or environmental context. Children, adults and families with the highest unmet need may therefore be missed, invited too late or directed to a pathway that does not fit their circumstances. A useful challenge solution must translate insight into timely enrolment, recall or follow-up through the appropriate Well-Child Visit, adult comprehensive screening or preventive pathway, while making uncertainty and authorized data use visible.

Objective

Use relevant health and genomic insights to identify children, adults and communities with unmet or elevated health risk and connect them, at the right time, to the appropriate Well-Child Visit, adult comprehensive screening or preventive intervention.

Your solution should

1
Transparent risk and care-gap identification. Use authorized clinical, genomic, developmental, behavioral, family-history, Well-Child Visit, adult screening and contextual data to identify elevated or unmet risk and missed or overdue care, showing the major factors behind each flag.
2
Personalized preventive pathways. Recommend the appropriate Well-Child Visit, adult comprehensive screening, test or preventive intervention, including timing, recall and follow-up, within approved guidelines, while clearly distinguishing population prioritization from individual clinical decision-making.
3
Culturally appropriate engagement. Tailor language, channel and message to children, parents, caregivers and adults, while giving individuals and families a meaningful choice about participation.
4
Local population safeguards. Assess reach, false-positive and false-negative patterns, recommendation rates and uptake across relevant segments of the local target population; suppress small cells and avoid misleading results when data is insufficient.
5
Outcome-based evaluation. Measure completed Well-Child Visits, adult comprehensive screenings and preventive actions, together with timely attendance, recall and follow-up completion, model calibration and downstream clinical value.

What makes a winning AI pitch?

A winning AI pitch centers on delivering **tangible value** rather than simply listing technical features (0:38:34). Stephan Geyer emphasizes that judges and stakeholders are interested in the **impact** your solution has on the target audience—whether they are clinicians, patients, or administrators.

To build a persuasive case, your pitch should include these elements:

*   **The "Wow" Moment:** Design your demo to show a clear, memorable highlight that proves the solution's effectiveness (0:39:34).
*   **Clear Narrative:** Structure your story so it is easy to follow, keeping the focus on the problem solved and the benefit gained (0:36:25, 0:37:51).
*   **Demo Readiness:** Ensure your demo is well-rehearsed with a clear script, a "happy path" that works flawlessly, and a plan for how you will handle potential questions from the judges (0:39:51-0:40:51).
*   **Measurable Outcomes:** Clearly define how the success of your solution will be measured (0:27:00).

Ultimately, a successful pitch bridges the gap between a high-level idea and a practical, actionable use case by proving that your tool solves a specific, significant pain point (0:10:25, 0:13:34).




What makes a good demo script?
A good demo script is essential for effectively showcasing your AI solution during a pitch. According to the "Demo Checklist" presented by Stephan Geyer (0:39:51), a strong script should focus on several core components:

*   **Structure and Narrative:** Start with a clear script that tells a compelling story rather than just clicking buttons (0:39:51). Ensure you include a "Happy Path"—a pre-planned, successful sequence that highlights your solution's core value without getting bogged down by errors or edge cases (0:40:08).
*   **The "Wow" Moment:** Design your demo around one, singular, impactful "Wow" moment that proves the value of your AI agent (0:39:34).
*   **Time Management:** Keep the demo concise. You should be able to deliver your primary message within 3 minutes, leaving ample room for Q&A (0:37:51).
*   **Preparedness for Q&A:** Anticipate the judges' questions. A good demo script includes a plan to handle inquiries about data, safety, technical feasibility, and the business model behind your innovation (0:40:17).

By following this framework, you ensure the demo serves its purpose: to provide concrete evidence of how your idea solves a specific, real-world problem.
